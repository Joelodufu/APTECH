### **Class Note for Sub-Session 1: Moving Average for a Given Time Period**

---

#### **1. Literature/Problem Statement (What Brought About the Concept):**

Before modern data analysis, people often relied on intuition or assumptions when making decisions. For example, business owners might believe their sales are increasing, or weather forecasters might assume a temperature trend is rising without actual evidence.

Small changes in data can naturally occur even when nothing significant has changed. This led to the development of the **moving average**, which allows us to smooth out data fluctuations and better understand underlying trends.

The **Moving Average** was created to help identify real trends and eliminate random noise or fluctuations. It's a powerful tool used across various fields, like finance, economics, and even weather forecasting, to help people make more informed decisions based on data rather than assumptions.

#### **3. Simple Definitions (For Dummies) for Terms Used:**

- **Moving Average (MA)**: A statistical technique that calculates the average of a set of data over a specific period of time to highlight trends and reduce fluctuations. It’s like taking a snapshot of a group of numbers to see the overall pattern.

- **Simple Moving Average (SMA)**: The most common type of moving average. It’s just the average of a certain number of data points.

- **Exponential Moving Average (EMA)**: A type of moving average that gives more weight to the most recent data points, making it more sensitive to changes.

- **Smoothing**: The process of averaging data to remove the effects of small fluctuations and show the underlying trend.

---

#### **4. Real-Life Use Cases:**

- **Stock Market Analysis**: Traders use moving averages to identify trends and signals for buying or selling stocks. For instance, when a short-term moving average crosses above a long-term moving average, it may be a signal to buy.

- **Weather Forecasting**: Meteorologists use moving averages to predict temperature trends and smooth out the day-to-day fluctuations in weather data.

- **Business and Sales**: A company may use a moving average to understand its sales trends over time, helping to predict future demand and make more accurate inventory decisions.

- **Manufacturing**: Moving averages help manufacturers track production output and quality over time, smoothing out the natural fluctuations in production rates.

---

#### **5. Micro Examples (For Concept Understanding):**

Let’s take a look at the **Simple Moving Average (SMA)** for a small dataset (item prices over 5 days):

| Day | Price ($) |
| --- | --------- |
| 1   | 10        |
| 2   | 12        |
| 3   | 15        |
| 4   | 18        |
| 5   | 16        |

To calculate the **3-day Simple Moving Average (SMA)**:

1. For Day 3:
   [ \text{SMA} = \frac{10 + 12 + 15}{3} = \frac{37}{3} = 12.33 ]

2. For Day 4:
   [
   \text{SMA} = \frac{12 + 15 + 18}{3} = \frac{45}{3} = 15
   ]

3. For Day 5:
   [
   \text{SMA} = \frac{15 + 18 + 16}{3} = \frac{49}{3} = 16.33
   ]

These moving averages help smooth out fluctuations and show us the underlying trend in the data.

#### **6. Integral (Applied) Example (With Scenarios):**

Let’s imagine you’re working for a company that sells products and you’ve been asked to analyze monthly sales data for the past year. You need to calculate the **6-month moving average** to identify the overall trend, excluding any month-to-month fluctuations.

Here’s the sales data:

| Month | Sales ($) |
| ----- | --------- |
| Jan   | 5000      |
| Feb   | 6000      |
| Mar   | 5500      |
| Apr   | 5700      |
| May   | 5800      |
| Jun   | 6200      |
| Jul   | 6400      |
| Aug   | 6500      |
| Sep   | 7000      |
| Oct   | 6800      |
| Nov   | 7100      |
| Dec   | 7200      |

**Step-by-Step Calculation for 6-month SMA**:

1. For the period Jan-Jun:
   [
   \text{SMA} = \frac{5000 + 6000 + 5500 + 5700 + 5800 + 6200}{6} = \frac{33200}{6} = 5533.33
   ]

2. For the period Feb-Jul:
   [
   \text{SMA} = \frac{6000 + 5500 + 5700 + 5800 + 6200 + 6400}{6} = \frac{34600}{6} = 5766.67
   ]

And so on for the remaining months.

This gives you a clear picture of the overall trend in sales, making it easier to spot patterns and make informed decisions.

#### **7. Step-By-Step Excel Practice (Classwork)**

**Steps for Calculating Moving Average in Excel**:

1. Open a new Excel sheet.
2. Enter your data (e.g., sales or stock prices) in a column.
3. For the **3-day Simple Moving Average**:

   - In the first cell (C3), enter the formula: `=AVERAGE(B1:B3)` to calculate the average of the first 3 days.
   - Drag this formula down to fill the rest of the cells for each subsequent day.

4. Use the **Data Analysis Toolpak** in Excel:

   - Click on **Data** in the toolbar.
   - Select **Data Analysis** → Choose **Moving Average**.
   - Enter your range of data and specify the number of periods for the moving average (e.g., 3 days or 6 months).

---

#### **8. Interpreting Results:**

After calculating the moving average, you can interpret the results by observing how the moving average changes over time:

- **Upward Trend**: If the moving average is consistently increasing, it indicates that the data is showing an upward trend.
- **Downward Trend**: A consistent decrease in the moving average suggests a downward trend.
- **Fluctuations**: If the moving average still fluctuates a lot, it indicates a lot of short-term volatility, and you may need to adjust the period or use an exponential moving average (EMA).
